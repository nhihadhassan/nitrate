# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Film lovers and friend groups use Nitrate on desktop and mobile to discover films, keep a personal diary, publish reviews and lists, follow one another, and run recurring movie clubs. They are usually browsing artwork, making a quick state change such as rating or saving a film, or coordinating a shared decision with friends.

## Product Purpose

Nitrate brings personal film tracking and social movie-night planning into one coherent product. It should make everyday film discovery and logging fast, while making communal moments such as voting, spinning the club wheel, revealing ratings, and choosing the next screening feel memorable.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, tactile, refined. The product should feel warm, editorial, responsive, and quietly premium, with film artwork carrying most of the visual drama.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a gaming dashboard, a neon sci-fi interface, an animation showcase, or a generic glassmorphic SaaS product. Avoid exaggerated 3D rotation, huge zoom effects, childish celebration effects, decorative cursor glows everywhere, long loading choreography, and motion that competes with readable film information.

## Design Principles

1. Film artwork is the protagonist. Interface depth and motion should frame posters and backdrops, never overpower them.
2. State changes must feel immediate. Ratings, likes, watchlist actions, votes, and navigation respond at once and use motion only to clarify what changed.
3. Earn expressive moments. Everyday controls remain restrained so the club wheel, winner reveal, and blind-rating reveal can feel distinctive.
4. Preserve familiar workflows. Improve the existing architecture and component vocabulary instead of inventing new ways to perform standard tasks.
5. Quality is perceived through consistency. Shared depth, timing, easing, focus, loading, and image behavior should make every surface feel designed by the same hand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

All core workflows must remain keyboard and screen-reader usable with visible focus states and strong contrast. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing pointer tilt, parallax, staggered travel, and unnecessary transforms while preserving immediate opacity or state feedback. Touch users receive clear pressed feedback and never depend on hover for information or actions.
